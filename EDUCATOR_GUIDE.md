# Educator Guide — Treasure Ten Island (Make Ten)

## Audience and session

- **Approximate age:** 6 (individual differences expected)
- **Session length:** about 8–12 minutes, replayable in shorter bursts
- **Prerequisites:** counting objects to 10; recognizing numerals 0–10 is helpful but practiced here
- **Co-play:** an adult can sit beside the learner, read prompts, and model counting empty spaces

## Learning objectives

1. Connect numerals to quantities from 0 to 10 using a ten-frame.
2. Compose 10 in multiple ways (number bonds), including 0+10 and 5+5.
3. Represent simple addition and subtraction within 10 with visible counters, a picture model, and a matching equation.

Explain with objects **before** treating equations as the only representation — the lesson always shows counters first.

## Activity walkthrough

### 1. Build a quantity
Learner fills a ten-frame to match a target numeral (including **zero** and **ten**). Feedback uses counting strategies (e.g., empty spaces), not instant full answer dumps on first miss. Tap/click slots or use **Add/Remove shell** buttons (keyboard: `+` / `-`).

### 2. Make ten
A locked starting collection is shown; the learner completes the second part so both parts total 10. Bonds covered include 0, 5, 7, 2, 9 as starting parts (≥5 bonds, including 0+10 and 5+5).

### 3. Story problems
Six guided original stories (add/sub within 10) plus a **final quest** combining skills (7 + 3 → make ten). Incorrect tries suggest strategies (count on / recount leftovers). Unlimited retry; no lives or timers.

## Differentiation (two scaffold levels)

- **Help level: Light** — hints name strategies without always stating the exact missing number.
- **Help level: Extra** — stronger counting tips (e.g., empty-space counts). Labels avoid “weak/advanced.”

## Model limitations (important)

- Ten-frames are a **didactic model**, not the only way children understand number.
- Subtraction never produces negatives in the app (clamped); real-world “owe” language is out of scope.
- We **do not claim** validated learning gains, diagnostic value, or universal age suitability.

## Accessibility notes for adults

- Controls ≥ 48 CSS px; visible keyboard focus; table alternative to the ten-frame
- `prefers-reduced-motion` respected; no autoplay audio; optional read-aloud with visual fallback
- Works without drag (buttons + keyboard)

## Offline follow-up

Place ten household objects in two groups. Ask: “I have 6 — how many more to make 10?” Sketch a ten-frame on paper.

## Content sources / references

These reputable sources inform the use of ten-frames, number bonds to 10, and object-before-symbol practice. Links are for adults, not a child outbound maze:

1. **National Council of Teachers of Mathematics (NCTM)** — principles supporting representation and conceptual understanding in early number.  
   https://www.nctm.org/Standards-and-Positions/Principles-and-Standards/

2. **UK Department for Education / NCETM — Number bonds and ten-frames in early maths** (mastery-oriented guidance on composing 10).  
   https://www.ncetm.org.uk/classroom-resources/cp-number-bonds-to-10/

What they support here: composing 10 with visual structure (ten-frame), connecting symbols to quantities, and practising bonds rather than only written drills.
